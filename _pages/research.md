## Supraglacial Lake Dynamics and Potential Drainage Cascades

In the summer months, surface melting of the Greenland ice sheet results in thousands of kilometer-scale lakes called supraglacial lakes. Some of these lakes drain into the ice sheet through surface fracture features. Can one lake drainage event induce or trigger a nearby lake to drain? By what mechanisms can these lake drainage events communicate with each other? I use remote sensing data combined with theoretical models to investigate if slip-based tensile stress perturbations can trigger upstream lake drainage events.

## Wind Perturbations in a Circumpolar Current-Subpolar Gyre System

![Ocean circulation placeholder](/assets/images/research-ocean.png){: .align-left style="width: 40%;"}

The Antarctic Circumpolar Current and adjacent subpolar gyres play a crucial role in global heat transport towards Antarctic ice shelves. The large-scale, Southern Hemisphere westerly winds have been growing stronger and shifting southward, in agreement with atmospheric carbon dioxide and ozone forcing. How will these wind changes alter the supply and locations of oceanic heat approaching Antarctica? How may the ocean accommodate changes in surface momentum input? I investigate how the ocean responds to changes in wind stress in an idealized setting using the MITgcm ocean model, with implications for understanding future ocean warming and ice-ocean interactions.

## Ice Fracture and Calving Mechanics

Ice shelf calving and glacier terminus retreat are major contributors to global ice mass loss. I develop theoretical frameworks to understand under what conditions ice fractures, with applications across different glacial environments—from floating ice shelves to marine- and land-terminating glaciers.

My work demonstrates that there is **no single universal calving law**, but rather a family of analytical stress-based calving criteria for different crack configurations (e.g. surface crevasses, basal crevasses, meltwater/saltwater).

## Bizarre Ice Shelf Geometries and Instabilities

During my undergraduate research, I investigated the enigmatic surface "rolls" on the Ellesmere Ice Shelf, a peculiar morphology that is not often seen on other ice shelves. What processes could have shaped their formation and persistence? Using theoretical models and finite element simulations (COMSOL), we explored whether these features could arise from a viscous buckling instability driven by sea ice pressure.
